App C · Productivity · Meetings

Granola

The AI notepad for back-to-back meetings — listens, drafts notes, extracts actions, syncs to your tools. The smallest harness surface in the matrix, and the easiest to map end-to-end.

Granola is the cleanest reverse-engineering target in the matrix because the job is small and sharp: sit in a meeting, listen, produce notes worth keeping. Everything in the harness exists in service of that one outcome. Whatever you can ship in 25 minutes for Granola becomes the template for the bigger ones — same six slots, just more boxes to fill in Harvey or Claude FS.

What it is

An AI notepad for meetings — not a meeting bot

Granola listens to a meeting on your machine, transcribes locally, and turns the messy spoken stream into the notes you would have wished you wrote. The user-visible surface is a notepad: you can type your own bullets while the meeting happens, and Granola enriches them with what was actually said, the decisions reached, and the action items, and syncs the result to the tools you already use (Notion, Slack, calendars, CRMs).

It deliberately does not join the call as a bot. That is a product decision with harness consequences: capture happens on-device, the network surface is narrower, and the privacy story is easier to defend.

Anatomy of the harness

Six slots, mapped to a meeting product

Same six-slot anatomy as the other two examples — re-stated in the vocabulary of a meeting workflow.

Agents · subagents

One main note-taker, a few quiet specialists

Granola is small enough that the main loop is the agent — a transcribe → enrich → summarize pipeline. But the high-value patterns become clearer when you split it into subagents:

  • Listener — runs while the meeting is live; produces a rolling structured transcript and detects topic changes.
  • Note-merger — combines the user's typed bullets with what was actually said; preserves the user's voice as the spine.
  • Action extractor — identifies decisions, action items, and owners; structured output only.
  • Follow-up drafter — proposes the email / Slack / Notion entry; never sends on its own.
Maps to .github/agents/*.md.
Skills

On-demand prompts for meeting moves

A skill is a prompt loaded when its trigger fires. Granola's skills are the moves that turn raw transcript into something usable:

  • summarize-meeting — produce a 5-bullet recap framed for someone who wasn't there.
  • extract-decisions — list every decision actually reached, with the alternatives discussed.
  • extract-actions — produce owner / item / due-date triples; never invent owners.
  • draft-followup — email or Slack-shaped draft from the decisions + actions.
  • find-prior-context — pull what was said in the last meeting with these participants on this topic.
Maps to .github/prompts/*.prompt.md.
Tools · scripts & MCPs

Audio in, productivity surfaces out

The agent itself doesn't reach into the world — it asks tools to. In Granola-shape:

  • Capture tool — on-device audio capture + diarization; never sends raw audio off-device unprompted.
  • Calendar MCP — read today's meetings, participants, prior notes for the same series.
  • Notion / Docs / Drive MCP — write the notes file into the workspace folder the user picks.
  • Slack / Email MCP — post the recap; always requires a human confirm step.
  • CRM MCP — log the meeting against the right account / opportunity for sales workflows.
  • Search — scoped to the user's own past notes; not the public web.
Maps to scripts/*.sh + mcp.json.
Hooks · governance

Privacy boundary, enforced not promised

Hooks bracket every tool call. They run regardless of what the model decided, and they are where Granola's "we don't send audio off your device" story is actually upheld:

  • On-device boundary — PreToolUse: deny any outbound call that includes raw audio bytes.
  • Send-confirm gate — PreToolUse: any post-to-Slack / email / Notion requires an explicit user confirmation token.
  • Participant-scope check — PreToolUse: write to the meeting's calendar invitees' workspace only.
  • PII redaction — PostToolUse: strip phone numbers / addresses / financial figures from drafts unless the user typed them.
  • Audit log — PostToolUse: record what was synced, where, and on whose meeting.
Maps to .github/hooks/{pre,post}-tool-use.sh.
Knowledge

Your meetings, your tools, lightweight

Granola's knowledge is mostly your stuff — there's no public corpus to retrieve over. The layers are about helping the agent recognise the room:

  • Prior notes — your past Granola outputs, indexed by participants & topic.
  • Calendar context — invitees, agenda lines, series title, the doc attached to the invite.
  • User glossary — project codenames, customer aliases, acronyms unique to your team.
  • Tool schemas — the shape of your Notion DB, your Slack channels, your CRM stages — so output lands in the right slot.
Maps to knowledge/* + a small typed index.
Memory

Voice, names, and what's still open

Three flavours, each with a different lifespan:

  • Series memory — facts about this recurring meeting: stable participants, ongoing threads, last week's open actions still pending.
  • User memory — the user's writing voice, preferred bullet style, default destination ("always Notion, not Docs"), people they refer to by first name.
  • Conversation state — what's been said in this single meeting; flushed at end-of-meeting except for what was promoted to series memory.
Maps to memory/SESSION.md + per-series store.
How they compose on one turn

From "meeting just ended" to a synced recap

A 30-minute product sync wraps. The user closes the laptop lid and walks to the next call. Here is the path the harness takes in the background:

  1. System prompt & rules load — Granola persona, on-device privacy stance, "preserve the user's voice."
  2. Skill match — meeting-end trigger loads summarize-meeting, extract-decisions, and extract-actions.
  3. Memory read — the user's writing voice + the series memory ("Sam usually owns disclosures").
  4. Knowledge retrieval — last meeting's notes for the same series; the user's glossary.
  5. Subagent delegation — listener already produced the transcript; note-merger combines it with the user's typed bullets; action extractor produces the owner/item/due triples.
  6. Tool calls — Notion MCP writes the page; CRM MCP logs the meeting against the right account.
  7. Hooks run — on-device boundary, PII redaction, send-confirm gate before the Slack post.
  8. Memory write — open actions append to series memory so they show up at the start of next week's call.
Trade-offs

Why this works, and where it breaks

Why this works

The job is narrow and the user is in the loop. The harness can be small because the surface is small, and the privacy story holds up because most of the heavy work happens before any tool call leaves the machine. The result feels "magic" precisely because the agent is doing almost nothing the user couldn't verify in five seconds.

Where it breaks

When the meeting is genuinely strategic — heavy on subtext, code-switching, multi-party disagreement — the action extractor either invents structure that wasn't really agreed, or hedges so hard the notes are useless. The harness also can't recover from bad capture: a noisy room, an unmic'd speaker on the far end, and the rest of the pipeline is downstream of garbage.

For your breakout

If you picked Granola — reverse-engineer this

  • System prompt: write the 1-page AGENTS.md that bakes in "preserve the user's voice" and the on-device privacy stance.
  • Skills: name three meeting skills; write the 5-step body for extract-actions (and the rule for refusing to invent an owner).
  • Tools: list the four tools you'd allow; for each, write the JSON shape it returns.
  • Hooks: draft the PreToolUse rule that refuses to post to Slack without an explicit confirm token.
  • Knowledge: sketch the four layers (prior notes · calendar · glossary · tool schemas) — and decide which is consulted first.
  • Memory: decide what gets promoted from a single meeting into series memory vs left to die in conversation state.
